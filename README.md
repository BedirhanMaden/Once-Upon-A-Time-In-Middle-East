
# Once Upon A Time In Middle East


The project seeks to provide a broader perspective regarding the two major incidents in
the Middle East: The nationalization of Suez Canal in Egypt and Abadan Refinery in Iran.
Throughout an engaging IOS application, users will start a virtual journey, unraveling the causes
and aftermaths of such life-changing events.


The application is consisted of two sections: The nationalization of Suez Canal and the
nationalization Abadan Refinery in Iran. In each section, a total number of ten items are displayed.
These items are either photographs of events, people or things that give user more insight about
the related incident than their descriptions. The photographs used have the license under public
commons; however, their sources are still available within the app.


As stated in Griswold et al. one of the key factors in the meaning making process is the
observant’s location, and location is not just a physical measure, it is guided by the physical
position of the observant and depends on the way the material is presented. To provide
assistance with the meaning making process, within both sections, the concept of an art gallery is
tried to be imitated, in other words, the materials are exhibited as if they are in a physical art
gallery. To create that eﬀect, materials are put in an order to form a chronological table that allows
users to obtain a broader perspective. Aside that, the material, in this case photographs, are
displayed within frames over a blank wall, combined with artificial shadow, just like a physical art
gallery. Also when user taps on a material, the material gets bigger and a description text
appears, which represent the increase of proximity to an object in a physical art gallery.

As mentioned before, within each section, a total number of ten materials are displayed,
and they are in chronological order. The Suez Canal exhibition begins from the construction of
Suez Canal and ends in the relocation of Temple of Dendur and Abu Simbel, which reveals the
reasons behind the nationalization and demonstrates its eﬀects on historical artifacts and tourism.
The titles of the materials displayed are as following for the Suez Canal section:



- The Construction of Suez Canal
- The Statue of Ferdinand De Lesseps
- The Construction of Aswan High Dam
- The Famous Speech of Nasser
- A Post Stamp About the Nationalization of Suez Canal
- Nasser After the Nationalization of Suez Canal
- Clearance Medal
- UNICEF: Now or Never
- The Relocation of Abu Simbel Temple
- The Temple of Dendur


The Abadan exhibition, on the other hand, begins with the nationalization of Abadan
refinery, and instead of featuring the reasons behind it, the exhibition mainly focuses on the
aftermath of the nationalization. Again, as in the Suez Canal exhibition, the materials presented
are in chronological order, and the titles of them are as following:- Abadan
- The Rashidian Brothers
- The Tiara of the Princess
- The Attempt of the Coup
- The Coup
- White Revolution
- The King of the Kings
- Shah and Tourism
- 2,500-Year Celebration of the Persian Empire
- Cyrus Cylinder

To create this app, various diﬀerent technologies were utilized. To create the frontend part
of the application, which is also referred as the user interface, I used a framework called SwiftUI,
which is Apple’s most recent tool for creating an user interface. For the backend part, which is
hidden behind the frontend and does the main job, I used Swift programming language combined
with the Xcode application. Also, to display the materials within the exhibitions, I used Google
Firebase to create a database, put these materials into the database and linked them to the
backend, which delivered them to the frontend and made them user-readable.
